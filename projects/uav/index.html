Autonomous Multipurpose UAV

A modular quadcopter I'm co-developing with Yash Kakade, designed in Fusion 360 and built through rapid PETG prototyping, with a cylindrical fuselage and a removable avionics bay.

The Goal

A 7-inch-class quadcopter that can carry swappable payloads, such as a camera, lidar, or environmental sensors, for different missions. It also has to stay easy to work on. Propulsion, a 6S battery, flight electronics, and a companion computer all have to fit in one fuselage without sacrificing structural integrity, cooling, component retention, or center of gravity.

Design

I own the mechanical architecture and structural design. The airframe uses a printed cylindrical fuselage with four bolt-on motor arms. The electronics mount to a separate sled that slides into the fuselage, so the whole avionics stack can come out for servicing instead of being rewired in place.

Every joint uses heat-set threaded inserts rather than screws driven straight into plastic, so parts can be taken apart and reassembled repeatedly during iteration without stripping.

Software & Autonomy

Yash leads the software: a Raspberry Pi companion computer running ROS 2 alongside an ArduPilot flight controller, with telemetry, health monitoring, a hot-pluggable payload system, and a web dashboard. The flight controller stays in charge of flight safety. More on the software side at itsyashk.github.io.

Where It's At

The drone has completed its first successful hover test. On the mechanical side, the current revision is being redesigned around the removable, serviceable avionics bay, using lessons from packaging the electronics in the first build.
